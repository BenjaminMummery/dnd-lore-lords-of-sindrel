h2. Watching Greysheath

_Morning and afternoon of the 32nd of Enean, year 4218 ([[The Soulwind Calendar | SC]])_

The Wonch returned to [[Radwasp House | Radwasp House]] exhausted after a night of gate records, broken doors, and chicken-based reconnaissance. Greysheath House sat opposite them on [[Merrylane | Ambassador Street]] like a respectable lie: still façades, drawn curtains, and just enough movement at the windows to prove someone was home.

[[:cedric | Cedric]] was acquainted with Greysheath's staff — [[:wahibre-ten-sutekh | Wahibre ten-Sutekh]] and [[:harsa-nefer | Harsa Nefer]]. Like him, the pair were Lithra [[Adjutant | adjutants]] attached to the house. He had seen nothing suspicious from either, though charcoal deliveries had been arriving with curious regularity. The Wonch watched one such run: a cart depositing charcoal into the scuttle under the direction of a young dwarf in Lithra grey - Wahibre, from cedric's description. Above, every chimney smoked, but strangely thinly — as though each fireplace burned with the minimum of fuel.

[[:nakoma-deathwalker-mor-got-ha | Deathwalker]] had a letter from [[:seer-demeletas | Seer Demeletas]], arranging a private meeting on the upper tiers. The invitation was flattering, specific, and poetic.

bq(in-world-document). You smiled as though the future were foreseen —<br>
Not mine to give, and yet I did not mind.<br>
I am not won by courtly art, but lean<br>
Toward candour, and a wit that does not hide.
<br>
On thirty-third Enean, when dusk has passed its prime,<br>
Come to the Pythiol, by the upper stair;<br>
A private chamber I have, dim lamps and bread and wine,<br>
And no one else required to witness there.
<br>
No one shall meet you at the seer's door;<br>
Within, all waits — the wine, the bread, the light.<br>
It seems a closed door never long keeps more<br>
Than passing trouble for you — push tonight.
<br>
I shall be hungry for your company,<br>
And pleased to find you difficult to ignore.
<br>
Demeletas

h2. The Bound Garden

Deciding to wait until nightfall before tackling Greysheath, the wonch turned their attention to other investigative paths. [[:sable-silvestre | Sable]]'s intelligence had been blunt: [[:silas-oestra | Silas Oestra]] was banned from [[The Bound Garden | the Bound Garden]] after an incident the proprietors described, with admirable vagueness, as a failure of manners toward the staff. The Wonch reasoned that a house which had seen Silas at his most private might know more about him than a formal Senate hearing ever would — and that [[House Oestra | Oestra]]'s summons could wait one more day if leverage was the currency they intended to spend.

The walk up the [[The Canted Terrace | Canted Terrace]] passed near the Belton Institute — site of the summoning the party had once suspected of masking sulfur theft — but today's destination lay elsewhere.

bq). The Canted Terrace does not announce itself. It sidesteps.
<br>
The walk up is all polished steps and iron lamps, ceremonial gutters, and carved stonework. The district sits high enough to be respectable and at an angle to everything fashionable — walled, screened, and spoken of in the careful tones people use for places they are not supposed to enjoy looking at. Coachmen know the routes. Factors mention addresses without blushing. [[Merrylane | Merrylane]], somewhere beyond the sightline tricks, pretends not to know which way you turned.
<br>
Incense reaches you before the name does — amber, musk, something sweet enough to make you forget what quarter you are in. Then the sign: modest on purpose, gilt on black lacquer. _The Bound Garden._ Beneath it, in smaller script: _Private Repose. By Introduction._
<br>
No painted nymphs. No winking cherubs. Only pierced bronze screens where windows might have been — arabesque cutouts spilling lamplight and haze onto the Terrace walk, with sultry music dimly audible behind them. _By introduction only_ was not a joke; the doorkeepers were polite but firm about barring entry until Demeletas's name was dropped.

They were admitted as Hexine investigators — people worth vouching for, if only because turning them away created paperwork somewhere else. The master of ceremonies received them in a quiet antechamber and explained the service with the calm of someone reading terms and conditions that had been litigated for centuries. Each guest would receive a private experience shaped to their deepest desire. Attendants would summon the appropriate companions. The house would not hurry them. The house would not judge them. The price, though steep, was well within the wonch's means.

The Wonch were separated, and the Garden began to answer them one by one.

[[:nakoma-deathwalker-mor-got-ha | Deathwalker]] died on a battlefield that smelled of gold light and distant victory. Old comrades gathered around her wounded body, held her hand, rested palms on her shoulders, and welcomed her as though nothing had been lost. The scene did not offer triumph so much as permission: recognition, companionship, and the chance to stop fighting at last among people who understood what she had endured.

[[:amanira | Amanira]] entered a library whose shelves rose beyond the room's apparent height. Its books answered every question with confident authority — and every answer was something she already believed. The Garden had taken her existing convictions and returned them in the voice of scholarship, making familiar ideas feel proven because they had been printed and bound.

[[:rossin-ros-greyhirst | Ros]] was offered gentler hospitality: a woman in flowing, natural colours, a tray of drinks that smelled of flowers and fungi and green growing things, and an invitation to explore the garden beyond the room without pressure or deadline. Ros drank nothing. After a long, patient silence she said, plainly, that the house could turn the illusion off now — and the garden door closed, the tray vanished, and the attendant across from her returned to her real shape, disappointed but professional.

[[:wilrin-racenglade | Wilrin]], inexperienced and curious, faced an impossible crowd — every age and body, all beautiful, all welcoming, all eager. The scene was not one fantasy but an abundance of them, inviting him to stop choosing and simply accept that everyone there wanted him.

[[:pin | Pin]] drank in a tavern with his old mercenary company: warm light, familiar back-slaps, the casual physical affection of people who had survived hard work together. For a while the room offered belonging without explanation.

[[:ayr | Ayr]] saw his own crew as well, but the reunion carried an extra temptation: [[:sable-silvestre | Sable]]'s ledger lay within reach, promising Black Cat secrets without another bargain. Comfort and kompromat braided into the same dream.

[[:silrie-aegiskiir | Silrie]] found herself invited to meditate with [[:raymond-quill | Raymond]] and [[:gillette-quill | Gillette Quill]]. They wore the robes of her order and performed its ceremonies with unsettling perfection. They spoke with practised solemnity about the wickedness they had done, the harm they had caused, and their hunger for a better path — and every question Silrie asked was turned back toward her own faith, her own duty, her own capacity to forgive. The brothers were not offering facts. They were offering the seductive possibility that the men might, with her help, become worthy of the people they pretended to be.

When Silrie insisted on seeing what lay beneath the illusion, the figures merged into a single pale demon. It did not snarl. It explained, with something like tired honesty, that it was bound to serve the visitors of the Bound Garden — that service was its purpose; that it had not chosen that purpose any more than mortals chose the restrictions of flesh, law, and gravity. Perhaps, it suggested, they all existed within the bounds of a summoning circle, just as it existed within the bounds of a mortal life. This was not an independent creature bound within magical restrictions, in some fundamental way it _was_ the restrictions - the terms of its summoning were its bones, not its chains.

One by one they returned to the first room, carrying very different impressions. The master of ceremonies confirmed what they had begun to suspect: the beautiful attendants were not actors. The Garden summoned demons, bound them for the appointment, shaped them to reflect the desires of the patron, and dismissed them afterward.

h2. Silas Oestra's Shame

Even for a client who had been dismissed in bad standing, the keeper was professionally insistent on maintaining [[:silas-oestra | Silas Oestra]]'s privicy. When framed as an explicit Hexine Circle investigation matter, however, they were more than happy to open up.

Silas had visited often, and his requests had been remarkably consistent: scenarios involving his father, in which he won approval by succeeding in contests, rescues, or other proofs of worth. The final visit had gone wrong. Silas chose a fencing match he was meant to win.. Instead he became violent and attempted to kill the manifestation outright. Rebuilding the attendant cost the house considerable resources. Silas was banned.

The same conversation produced a secondary lead. [[:regnif-d-log | Regnif d'Log]] had used the Garden's services once, roughly two months earlier, and left after only a few minutes. The keeper could not say why — only that d'Log had found the experience more intense than he had prepared for.

h2. The Outfit and the Coal Cart

On the way back through the upper tiers, Deathwalker collected the outfit she had arranged for her date with Demeletas. The Wonch returned to Radwasp with their dignity in various states of repair, their heads full of demons and desire, and a clearer picture of Silas Oestra as a man who shouted at his father even when his father was a conjured illusion.

They did not have long to reflect. The charcoal cart was due at Greysheath, and the party had decided to use it.

[[:amanira | Amanira]] would take an animal form, slip through while the delivery was made, and open an upper window. The rest would enter by the routes she created, with the house's attention drawn toward the front. It was the sort of plan that sounded elegant in a parlour and less elegant once you were crouched behind a coal scuttle in failing light.

As dusk settled, the cart arrived. The dwarf emerged from the service side of the house. Charcoal began to pour. The plan moved from discussion to action.

h2. Into Greysheath

The infiltration split into several groups almost immediately — which was either good tradecraft or the Wonch's habitual inability to stay in one place, depending on who you asked.

[[:pin | Pin]], [[:ayr | Ayr]], and [[:wilrin-racenglade | Wilrin]] searched the basement. They found themselves surrounded by the rude realities of running a household - a servants' hall with empty beds and foot lockers, a water closet kept with unsettling cleanliness, and a charcoal store whose outer hatch opened from the street. Deeper in, a wine cellar, a butler's pantry that doubled as a servants' sitting room, and a clerical office whose lack of personal effects was almost as suspicious as its ledgers. Among the papers, Wilrin found a rough map of [[Sindrel | Sindrel]] — the sort of thing an honest steward might keep, and the sort of thing a cartographer-spy might keep for other reasons.

During their search they ran headlong into Wahibre ten-Sutekh - the dwarven servent who had overseen the charcoal delivery. In a tense exchange, Wahibre was successfully persuaded that they did in fact have a right to be there as part of their investigation, and that they should be permitted to go about their business unaccosted. He was the unceremoniously locked in a cupboard, although not before Wilrin had pocketed several jars of jam.

Above them, [[:nakoma-deathwalker-mor-got-ha | Deathwalker]] ascended a drainpipe with [[:kithrak-ii | Kithrak II]] and [[:tama | Tama]] in tow, landing in a drawing room with more flair than stealth. [[:rossin-ros-greyhirst | Ros]] and [[:silrie-aegiskiir | Silrie]] gained the roof and, through the windows, saw a woman matching Sable's description of the Snake ascending toward the upper landing and likely to encounter Deathwalker. A thrown slate and a dart made noise in the garden. It was not enough to pull the newcomer away from the landing. Ros and Silrie watched from above as Greysheath woke to the fact that it was no longer empty.
