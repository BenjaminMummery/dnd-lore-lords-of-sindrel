h2. Into the Deep Archives

Once protected, the party returns to the catacombs and continues past the Crossroads. The route passes through the following displays:

* The Seven-Day War diorama.
* The Founding of the Magedom of Houd.
* The Hall of Failed Resurrections.
* The Extinct Menagerie.
* The Hall of Plagues.
* The Hall of the First Watch.

The dragonborn skull Wilrin carries was part of a display in the Hall of Failed Resurrections. Its accession record marks it as **on loan to Fallowhurst College**. This gives the party a concrete connection between the Archives and Fallowhurst, and may cause the curators to classify Wilrin as an overdue borrower or stolen exhibit.

Pin's sword belongs in the Hall of the First Watch. The caretakers treat it as overdue and may attempt to reclaim it, demand its return chit, or ask Pin to provide a replacement witness for the collection.

Both objects may trigger archive-curator complications. The caretakers are precise and bureaucratic rather than conversational: they may request catalogue numbers, accession chits, return dates, or the names of the lending officers before escalating.

h3. Seven-Day War diorama

bq). The passageway opens into an enormous space - the walls and ceiling almost lost in the haze of distance. The floor rises and falls in a stone simulacrum of rolling hills, you can even see the glint of the meagre light reflecting from the waves of what appears to be a river. You realise that you are surrounded by a motionless army of armed and armoured skeletons. Or rather, as you look closer, two armies frozen in the throes of a colossal conflict. A macabre diorama whose grandiose effect is spoiled only slightly by the fact that many of the skeletons are missing parts - the floor is littered with teeth and fingerbones that, presumably, were once part of the display. A clear path demarcated by bands of silver laid into the dark stone of the floor, wends its way between the posed skeletons.

bq). The uneven floor is rent by a semicircular chasm that arcs outwards from the wall. On the other side you can see the exit - a doorway identical to the one through which you entered, surrounded by thronged skeletons (in Tellerite armour). Spanning the chasm is a narrow bridge, apparently made of brass, atop which stands the skeleton of a giant. A club the size of a carthorse is clasped in the colossus's hand, and it is posed with its back to you as if denying the crowded skeleton passage across the bridge.

There is room to cross between the giant's legs. Using the bridge destabilises the skeleton and causes the club to fall - require a saving throw.

h3. Founding of the Magedom of Houd

bq). The floor beneath you is carved into a relief map of northern [[Artresh | Artresh]]. It is a patchwork of various stones and metals, as if assembled from some colossal jigsaw of mismatched construction. It is positioned so that [[Soulwind | Soulwind]] would stand at the exact centre of the room, but in its place is a raised dias with a tableau - a score of statues each made of a different material stand around a table. On the opposite side of the room behind a waist-high reproduction of the [[Arkay Mountain Range | Arkay Mountains]] you can see an elaborate golden arch, its decorative ornamentation forming the silhouette of the city of [[Sindrel | Sindrel]].

The tableau depicts the founding of the [[The Magedom of Houd | Magedom of Houd]]. The different materials represent the various kingdoms that united - history rolls to recognise their old borders.

h3. Hall of the First Watch

bq). The hall is built like a Meness courthouse enlarged for the dead. Pale stone, black timber crossbeams, a central aisle flanked by ranked alcoves rather than pews. In each alcove kneels a skeleton in the remains of watch-coats and garrison armour, short swords or tower shields held with ritual stiffness. The plaques record rank and date of death.

The planks record rank and date of death.

Several alcoves are missing swords or shields.

h3. The Hall of Failed Resurrections

bq). The corridor ends at a pair of white doors, their surfaces engraved with dozens of names.
<br>
Beyond is a long, cold gallery of glass cases and articulated frames. The bodies within are displayed as speimins of a strange and dangerous art. One corpse casts two shadows in the flickering light of your torch. Another has been reassembled with the bones of three different species. A woman sits upright beneath a veil, her flesh perfectly preserved on one side and reduced to clean ivory on the other.
<br>
Every case bears a narrow brass plate: a name, an injury, a date, and an outcome. *Failure. Failure. Failure.* The word repeats down the hall until it becomes less a judgement than a rhythm.

bq). One tall case stands empty but for a metal frame that once held a body upright. Etched into the glass behind where the skull would stand is a design you recognise as a partial version of a binding sigil. A plaque reads **ON LOAN — FALLOWHURST COLLEGE OF MEDICINAL STUDIES. RETURN TO ARCHIVE PENDING.**

The curators classify Wilrin's skull as an overdue exhibit. If he refuses to surrender it, a caretaker may begin the return procedure; if he presents himself as the skull's current bearer, the archive may instead demand an explanation, a loan extension, or a replacement accession.

h3. The Extinct Menagerie

bq). The next chamber opens with the scale and silence of a grand natural-history museum. Black stone rises into a vaulted darkness, and pale skeletons hang from the ceiling in poses of impossible motion: a six-winged predator banking through the air, a long-necked grazer bending toward an absent meadow, a serpent with plated ribs coiled around a dead tree.
<br>
The displays continue below: claws arranged in drawers, jaws mounted on silver frames, eggshells nested in velvet, and enormous footprints set into the floor like paving stones. Some creatures are recognisable only from illustrations on their plaques. Others have no surviving name at all. Their labels use phrases such as *last confirmed specimen*, *habitat lost*, and *extinct by the third century*.
<br>
The room should be lifeless. Instead, small movements keep occurring at the edge of vision. A tail settles against a plinth. A rib cage expands and contracts once. Somewhere above, something with too many joints moves carefully across the beams.
<br>
At the far end, an empty display case stands beneath a fresh brass label. The label has no name, only a blank accession number and the words: **SPECIMEN EXPECTED.**

The Archive is not only preserving extinct life. It is still waiting for new additions. Caretakers may attempt to catalogue a living character, record a fragment of their clothing or blood, or politely request that the party stop disturbing the displays.

h3. The Hall of Plagues

bq). The air changes before the door is fully open. It smells of vinegar, old linen, and something sweet that has gone wrong. Frost rims the threshold, but the frost does not melt beneath your breath.
<br>
The gallery beyond is divided by tall panes of greenish glass. Behind them stand physicians, nurses, patients, and officials in various stages of decay, each preserved beside the instrument, cloth, or object associated with their final illness. Some sit in beds. Some kneel over bowls. One figure is frozen with a hand pressed over its mouth, fingers spread as though it had just remembered what it was carrying.
<br>
Brass plaques identify the outbreaks by name, location, and mortality. Several list no cure. Others record cures that were discovered too late to matter. Along the walls, rows of sealed jars contain blackened lungs, pale nodules, bundles of hair, and things too small to identify. Each jar is marked with a warning in three different scripts.
<br>
At the centre of the room, a circular table supports a single empty chair. A physician's skeleton sits behind it, quill poised above an open ledger. Its skull turns as you enter.
<br>
"State the symptoms," it says. "State the date of exposure. State the names of those already dead."

The Hall is not necessarily an immediate combat encounter. The physician may ask questions, offer a diagnosis that is centuries out of date, or identify a character's magical wound as part of a disease no living scholar remembers. Opening a reliquary or disturbing a sealed display should trigger the caretakers' contamination protocol.

h2. The Quarantine Zone and the Sacrum

The party emerges from the deep archives into the Quarantine Zone. The Zone should feel like a dead city preserved at the instant of catastrophe, with no bodies despite every other detail remaining in place.

h3. Entrance from the Wallarch Breach

bq). The iron boundary rises in a line too straight to be natural: black metal banded with bronze, ward-glyphs repeating at intervals no craftsman would choose without instruction, maintenance scaffolds clinging to the face like lichen on a wound that refuses to close.
<br>
Down a side street, around the back of a warehouse, through a gap between buildings that was never meant to be a path, gradually the crowded bustle of Wallarch fades into the background and you find your way to the breach. A break in the wall, a narrow crack that runs vertically, a hair's breadth at the top, barely wider enough for a person at the base. It's a squeeze. Deathwalker needs to turn sideways.
<br>
On the other side, the Quarantine Zone stretches away. Deserted streets. Houses whose blank windows regard you like empty eye sockets. All shrouded in deathly silence. Nothing moves, not a bird, not a rat, not a single sign of life can be seen.
<br>
Standing within it, you understand why the Quarantine Zone is the stuff of horror stories and childish nightmares. The air has a weight to it - not heat, not cold, but pressure at the temples, as though the district ahead were watching you from directions geometry does not admit. Your skin registers it before your mind names it: a wrong place. A dead place.

h3. Within the Zone

bq). The further you go, the more the schizophrenic architecture of Sindrel gives way to an unfamiliar pattern. Dark stone and pointed arches, gargoyles worn smooth by centuries of bad weather and worse magic, skull motifs chosen with the restraint of people who thought they were being tasteful. Streets lie where they fell: courts, ossuary gates, memory-archives with their doors hanging open, hospitals and academies and civic halls all arranged with the terrible patience of a civilisation that believed death was a transition, not an ending.
<br>
That belief lingers in the stones. The Zone feels less abandoned than unresolved — as though someone left in the middle of a sentence and the punctuation never arrived. In places the necromantic residue is visible: a faint glow on lintels, frost that is not cold, dust that arranges itself into ridges no wind could have made.
<br>
Somewhere ahead — at the heart of the wound, where the public story says the [[The Eighth House | Eighth House]] died — the architecture grows denser, more deliberate, more official. If the Zone is a scar, that is the place the blade went deepest.

Wisdom check — the thing you _should_ be seeing but aren't. Every detail of the quarantine zone remains as it was left in a single instant (the food has desiccated rather than rotting - not even bacteria lives here). But there are no bodies.

h3. The Sacrum

bq). You know the building before anyone names it. The Sacrum.
<br>
It sits at the centre of the Quarantine Zone like a verdict - flying buttresses of black stone, pointed arches stacked upon pointed arches, gargoyles festoon the lintels. Skull motifs here are not decoration; they are punctuation. A grand sprawling mass of halls, archives, and spires.
<br>
The doors are grand. The windows are narrow.. No lamp burns at the entrance. The Sacrum is dark, and cold, and hungry.

bq). The air is dry and colder than the Zone outside — preserved, not fresh. Corridors branch into memorial galleries where niches wait in ranks, not the compact alcoves of the catacombs but spaces in which a full skeleton could stand. Floors are black polished stone. Vaulting draws the eye upward into shadow, then denies it a resting place. Every room feels like the antechamber to a larger grief.
<br>
There is no bustle of a living headquarters — no clerks, no petitioners, no polite necromantic commerce. What remains is worse than emptiness: the sense of duties half-finished. The necromantic pressure is strongest here — not the diffuse wrongness of the streets, but a focused weight, as though the building itself were still trying to complete a ritual interrupted a thousand years ago.

The party will need magical protection to get here. Luckily, [[Kelferria the Smith | Kelferria]] has plenty to hand.
