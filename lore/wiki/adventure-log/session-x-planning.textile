h2. Upcoming Session Plan (Session 19)

h3. Overview

Last time we got through the archives, so we begin just after the extinct menagerie.

The next room is actually a side room off the main path — light and movement behind an ajar door will attract them. Behind is a research office: a much more advanced curator (spells inscribed into the entire skeleton like scrimshaw) has been actively researching the archives' next expansion — the **Hall of Successful Resurrection**. In particular, it has collated records on [[:nakoma-deathwalker-mor-got-ha | Deathwalker]] — reports and accounts meant to reconstruct her life between **Brassmeade Bridge** and the present. The Anathemists **disabled** it (too observant to dodge reliably). Beats: **[[:amanira | Amanira]]** learns DW fought on the **opposite** side of the [[World History | Seven Day War]] (she need not reveal herself as Tellarite); **[[:pin | Pin]]** finds evidence DW was involved in the destruction of mercenaries he knew (Black Bloods / **Corvus** thread — see curator files).

From there we emerge into the Quarantine Zone. A bit of exploration, discovering that the zone is deserted, dead, and devoid of bodies. As we head toward the sacrum we find more traces of the anathemists - there's a trail between the wall breach and the sacrum where they've moved materials.

Entering the sacrum we find Nielsenn's corpse and ghost. This can fill in the full story of the anathemist heist, and urge vengeance on his killers.

We also get some hints towards House Samhain's fate as the party explore.

Finally we meet Paddington. He's waiting for the party with a few Anathemist allies. Boss fight — details under *Grand Hall* below.

Lastly, the defeated Paddington mocks them with his final words - he was just the distraction; the bombs are almost in place by now and there's nothing the adventurers can do to stop them. Dockward will fall. We end on a cliffhanger as a bomb detonates far overhead.

Following session: the adventurers race against time to disable the bombs. Having been pressured into action, the anathemists are less prepared and the bombs are not syncronised - the detonation at the end of the last session was early. The wonch save the day!

h3. The Research Office

**Placement:** Side door off the **Extinct Menagerie** exit corridor — flicker of **working light** attracts the eye.

bq). The room is too small to be a hall and too tidy to be storage. The walls are lined with shelves stuffed with documents. A chalkboard bears what you recognize as a map of this section of the archives, one section has been labeled in mechanically perfect copperplate: "Phase XIV Expansion - The Hall of Resurrection". Low tables around the room are home to ordered stacks of documents and notes.
<br>
At the centre, a curator-skeleton sprawls in a high backed chair, its entire surface scrimshawed with necromantic script. The sternum plate has been **cracked** — deliberate, recent — and the light in its eye sockets is out. In familiar chalk on the floor at its feet are the words "Do not reactivate".

**The curator (disabled):** **Preparator-tier** — scrimshaw inscriptions from crown to metatarsals. Anathemists **struck the sternum inscription** (too observant; tracked their chalk and rerouted patrol logic). **Reactivate:** clean fracture + Arcana or Religion DC 15 → speaks with greater precision and complexity than the other curators; treats party as **visiting scholars** until cases are touched; will attempt to **file** Deathwalker as exhibit candidate if she stands still too long in the light.

The table next to the curator holds the Deathwalker dossier

bq(in-world-document). Dossier on provisional accession target D-NR-1701. Compiled for consideration as central exhibit for The Hall of Resurrection.
<br>
Pre-3958 information is fragmentary. Descriptions of the subject's tattoos indicate membership of one of the mountain goliath clans, likely clan Mor'got'ha. As goliaths are typically poor record keepers, it is necessary to compile information primarily from the accounts of their foes. These are often laden with expletives and self justification. During this period, accounts of a series of skirmishes with Tellerite Expiditionary Forces provide the majority of our data. Earlier research concluded that the reported chants of *Deathbringer* from the goliaths was a simple warcry, and that accounts of a general of unusual skill and daring were either propoganda or the excuses of the ignominiously defeated. The few more lucid reports, however, are consistent in describing a single Mor'got'ha warrior at the head of the goliath battalions.
<br>
Of more concrete provenance is the membership of the Combined Interspecies Defence Batallion formed in 3958 to oppose the Tellarite invasion. The rosters include a small band of goliaths under the command of a 'Deathbringer'. It was this band that, vastly outnumbered, held the Brassmeade Bridge against the bulk of Tellerite reinforcements for a full 10 days, undoubtably swaying the decisive battle on the plains of Hermann in favour of the Houdians.
<br>
Tellarite accounts make reference to an "undying giant" who refused to yield even when standing alone against their massed armies. _Note - this is likely the source of the later narrative placing a single giant on the bridge. Curators should be retasked to update the Brassmeade diorama_. The death of this final warrior is attributed to the disgraced Tellerate battle-necromancer Nitherfang Gilfrarrin.
<br>
During the Tellerite retreat, multiple corroborated reports describe Gilfarrin, hardly a reliable part of the Tellerite force at the best of times, separating from his retreating regiment and cutting a bloody swath across southern Houd with the help of his bodyguard/executioner. Descriptions of his companion indicate a heavily scarred goliath with an apparently preternatural immunity to harm. This apparently ended with the necromancer's death - a headless body reliably identified as Gilfarrin was discovered in the woods of Goatheim in 3965. Of his companion there was no sign.
<br>
Fragmentary accounts from within the withdrawing Tellerite empire indicate that in the years following Nitherfang's death an unknown entity began hunting and killing Tellerite necromancers. Whether this was the action of a single individual or a network remains unclear, although the few survivor's accounts available indicate an unstoppable force that shrugged off everything up to and including siege weaponry.
<br>
Post 4000 the figure of an undying goliath warrior carrying a desiccated head begins to appear in accounts of battles across Artresh. While many of these can be ascribed to simple rumour an folk tails, some, such as the warrior who fought alongside the forces of the Mad King of Corvus in 4217 and single-handedly dispatched the Black Blood mercinaries, are well attested and corroborated. Many of these attach the epithet 'Deathwalker'.
<br>
Our inference is that these events describe a singular individual, a goliath of unusual martial prowess somehow resurrected by a Tellarite necromancer with her knowledge and will intact. That the spell has apparently outlived its caster's death by several centuries is equally intreating. This individual should be procured for study at the earliest opportunity.

h3. The Grand Stair

bq). The route makes its way up a grand and winding staircase. h3. The Quarantine Zone


bq). You emerge into a silent street, the sun obscured by a wall of low, dense cloud that makes the afternoon feel like the depths of evening. The street looks like any other in sindrel, but completely deserted. The silence hangs oppressively over everything.

Any houses they search are untouched, and deserted. Food remains on plates where it left - desiccated rather than rotted, not even bacteria live in this dead place. There are no bodies.

bq). The further you go, the more the schizophrenic architecture of Sindrel gives way to an unfamiliar pattern. Dark stone and pointed arches, gargoyles worn smooth by centuries of rain, skull motifs appearing with greater and greater regularity. Residential streets give way to more formal ceremonial patterns - plazas, audatoria, and mausoleums dominate.

h3. The Sacrum Entrance (Nielsenn)

**Building:** The [[Quarantine Zone | Sacrum]] — [[The Eighth House | House Samhain]] headquarters ([[Quarantine Zone | quarantine-zone]]). Anathemist **staging ground**: charcoal, nitre, sulphur traces, **armour prep**, copied routes, **disguised** traffic (not lived-in).

bq). You know the building before anyone names it. It sits at the centre of the Quarantine Zone like a verdict — flying buttresses of black stone, pointed arches stacked upon pointed arches, gargoyles on the lintels. The doors are grand; the windows are narrow. No lamp burns at the entrance. The heart of the Eighth House is dark, cold, and hungry.

**Interior (approach):**

bq). The air is dry and colder than the Zone outside — preserved, not fresh. Corridors branch into memorial galleries where niches wait in ranks, spaces in which a full skeleton could stand. Floors are black polished stone. Vaulting draws the eye upward into shadow, then denies it a resting place. Every room feels like the antechamber to a larger grief. Duties half-finished — not emptiness, but **interrupted ritual**.

**First corpse:** [[:nielsenn | Nielsenn]] — [[House Mabon | House Mabon]] **[[Adjutant | adjutant]]**, master locksmith (public record: vaporised in his shop; **true fate** GM-only until ghost speaks).

* **Body:** Desiccated, slumped against wall.
* **Ghost:** Manifests on approach — spirit **trapped** by Zone radiation ([[:nielsenn | Nielsenn]] character GM block). Wants **violence** against his killers (Georges dead; **[[:jean-de-dinteville | Paddington de Dinteville]]** still live at this point in Session 19 plan).

**Story the ghost can tell** (party-facing — [[The Investigation | investigation]] timeline; Nielsenn speaks in **broken time**, clutching at the last night):

* *1st Ostory 4218* - Aourorious posed as a concerned lock buyer - asked what the great and good of the city used to be sure he was getting the best. Bought a very fancy lock.
* *8th Ostory 4218* — Taken from his [[House Mabon | Mabon]] shop after dusk; **staged explosion** (public record: vaporisation). Woke in the Sacrum; **[[:marceline | Marceline]]** never showed her face — only merchants and a **gnome-sized** iron collar he could not pick from the inside.
* *8th–24th Ostory* — Held in a **memorial side-chapel** (sleep on stone, meals pushed under the door). Heard the anathemists moving heavy things; counted **seventeen days** by scratches on the wall.
* *25th Ostory — heist night*:
** just before midnight — **[[:georges-de-selve | Georges]]** and **[[:jean-de-dinteville | Paddington]]** took him through the [[The Wallarch District | Wallarch]] breach in painted chest-plates; catacombs → [[Osteomantic Archives | archives]] → **[[House Oestra | Leageur]]** emergence.
** Deep night — **[[:silas-oestra | Silas Oestra]]'s** rooms via stolen key; hours copying; Nielsenn heard them whisper a name — **Kosmios** — and did not understand it.
** Before dawn — Catacombs back to Sacrum; Georges held his arms; Paddington cut his throat here against this wall. Georges is dead (Greysheath); Jean still in the city (Paddington).
* *Aftermath* — Body abandoned; spirit **pinned** by Zone weight. He has watched **merchant traffic** come and go; knows **bombs** remain in the stockpile and **Dockward** is named on route scraps.


**Clues:** remaining **explosives** stockpile (not all deployed — Dockward climax still live); Nielsenn's **prosthetic finger** mechanism (loot as evidence for Kelferria/Mabon).

h3. The Grand Hall (showdown with Paddington)

**Setup:** **[[:jean-de-dinteville | Paddington de Dinteville]]** waits with **Anathemist allies** (stay-behind **merchant muscle** — **not** Georges, Marceline, or Arouraios). **Fight to the death** unless the table wants a captured lieutenant.

bq). The grand hall opens like a cathedral of death. Black stone columns rise into vaults where no choir ever sang; niches stand in ranks, each sized for a standing skeleton that never arrived. Wide steps rise to a grand throne sillhouetted against a window of blood-red glass.
<br>
Crates and bags are piled haphazardly, you can smell the sulfur from here - the raw materials of the bombmaker.
<br>
The dark shape of the throne shifts as the person sitting in it rises. As they step into the light you recognise Paddington de Interville. He wears Kelferria's plate over his merchant garb - the remains of a painted Oestra crest still visible on the chest.Similarly garbed spearmen step from behind pillars. Paddington spreads his hands. "Ah. The investigators. Just in time."

**Arena:** Columns, niches, crates (half cover). Necromantic pressure **maximum** — joined **dragon-seal** tattoos strongly urged; somatic casting costs as Session 18.

h4. Encounter — six level 4 PCs

**Target difficulty:** **Medium**, shading **Hard** if the party is fresh and casters open with big spells (environment + **Anathema's Fury** tax). **Not** a long-rest slugfest after the deep archives.

Lair hazards and **Zone pressure** below add threat without XP.

**Paddington tweaks for this fight:**

* **HP 58** (Greysheath wounds; was 68) — still dangerous, less slog.
* **Opens round 1:** bonus **Hide** behind crates if he won initiative, else **Flash-Powder Courtesy** centred on the **crate gap** (blind spreaders; soldiers shut eyes).
* **Priority targets:** concentration casters → whoever reached Nielsenn's corpse (Cutting Appraisal on the "hesitant" PC).
* **After You** only when a **soldier** is adjacent — never waste it on the Lieutenant unless desperate.
* **No flight** — side doors barred; win by killing the Wonch or dying loud enough that Dockward hears.

**Goon roles:**

| Name (optional) | Stat | Position | Job |
|-----------------|------|----------|-----|
| **Lieutenant "Steady Tread"** | Lieutenant | **Crate gap** (centre choke) | **Zealous Command** when casters slip up; longsword; does not chase past charcoal spill |

**Map (rough — 40 ft × 50 ft hall):**

```
[SOUTH — party enters, rubble difficult 10 ft]
        |    charcoal spill (difficult)     |
   niche|  crate|crate| GAP |crate|crate  |niche
   door |  half cover line (Paddington)   | door
        |    nitre cask · sulphur sacks   |
[NORTH — barred service doors, Dockward crates marked]
```

* **Half cover:** painted **Oestra** crates (3-high wall). Investigation DC 12: **Mabon** plate underneath — same kit from Nielsenn's room.
* **Difficult terrain:** **Charcoal spill** (15 ft patch west); **rubble** at entry.
* **Hazards (one-shot each unless noted):**
# *Nitre cask / sulphur* (east): first fire or spark (flash powder counts) within 5 ft → each creature in 10 ft radius DEX DC 12 or 2d6 fire; area lightly obscured 1 round.
# *Hanging iron lamp* (15 ft up, mid-room): one action, Athletics DC 14 or attack AC 12 → drops on 5 ft square, DEX DC 13 or 2d6 bludgeoning and prone.
# *Trip line + caltrops* in crate gap (Paddington prep): Perception DC 14 to spot; first creature crossing without disarming (Thieves' Tools DC 13) → DEX DC 13 or 1d6 piercing, prone, speed 0 until stand.

**Zone pressure (everyone in hall, start of each round):**

* **Plate (Pin/Ayr):** no effect.
* **Amulets (Amanira/Ros/Silrie):** CON DC 11 or **speed −10 ft** until end of turn (pressure, not exhaustion).
* **Joined tattoos (DW/Wilrin):** if **not** joined, CON DC 12 or **1d6 necrotic**; somatic spells while split cost **1 exhaustion** as Session 18.

**Lair actions (initiative count 20, 1/round, Paddington still alive):**

# **Smoke pot** — 20-ft-radius **lightly obscured** centred on crate fort (2 rounds).
# **Kicked keg** — charcoal keg rolls 20 ft toward largest PC cluster; DEX DC 12 or **1d8 bludgeoning** + 10-ft patch becomes difficult terrain.
# **Set spears** — until next lair action, first creature moving through **crate gap** from south: attacked as **+4 to hit, 2d6+2 piercing**; on hit, movement ends (brace).
# **Echo of the hall** — necromantic reverberation; one random PC WIS DC 11 or **disadvantage on next attack** (whispers of unfinished excarnation rites).

Rotate in order; skip if redundant (don't stack smoke).

**Round-by-round AI:**

# Lieutenant + soldiers **hold the gap**; spears at anyone in 10 ft. Paddington **Hand Crossbow** or **Flash-Powder** through crate slats.
# If two+ PCs flank: Soldier C **Dash** to intercept; Paddington **Cutting Appraisal** on healer or wizard.
# Bloodied Paddington: **Cunning Action Disengage** back, second **Flash-Powder**, monologue beat *Wonch* while Lieutenant **Zealous Command** punishes last spellcaster.
# Last goon dying: Paddington goes **melee** only if cornered; otherwise crossbow and insults until dropped.

**Scaling:**

* **Too easy** (party unhurt round 3): Lieutenant calls **Smoke pot** early + Soldier B closes to melee; or add **1 soldier** (raw +200, adjusted +400 — still Medium).
* **Too hard** (two down): Paddington offers false parley one round (no action) — use only if table needs breather; he lies about Dockward timer.
* **Short on time:** Drop to Paddington + **2 soldiers** (raw 1,500 ×2 = **3,000** adjusted — lower Medium); keep lair actions.

h3. Seven-Day War diorama

bq). The passageway opens into an enormous space - the walls and ceiling almost lost in the haze of distance. The floor rises and falls in a stone simulacrum of rolling hills; you can even see the glint of meagre light on what appears to be a river of polished obsidian. You are surrounded by a motionless army of armed and armoured skeletons - two hosts frozen in the throes of a colossal conflict. A path of silver bands wends between the ranks. Many are missing parts; teeth and fingerbones litter the slopes like hail that never melted. The remains of brass pegs, wire through rib cages, and discreet pins at the ankles can be seen where these supports have failed. The whole battlefield has the brittle patience of a display that expects to be looked at, not walked through.

bq). The uneven floor is rent by a semicircular chasm that arcs outward from the wall. On the far side, an exit doorway stands identical to the one behind you - set back into a steep hillside and framed by the bristling spears of a tightly packed tellarite phalanx. Spanning the chasm is a narrow bridge of brass plates, each riveted separately to the next; the flickering light reveals subtle pitting and corrosion at joins. At the center of the span is a colossal skeleton posed mid-denial: the skeleton of a giant with its back to you, hips and spine joined by visible armature, a club the size of a carthorse gripped in both hands as though arrested halfway through a swing. Above the giant, almost lost in the dark, brass cables climb into darkness.

bq). The path leads between the giant's legs, just wide enough for single-file.

The Anathemists crossed light and fast — minimal vibration, no destabilisation. The Wonch in [[:kelferria-the-smith | Kelferria]] plate are less subtle; treat the crossing as a **fixed procession order** (one spotlight beat per party member). Allies may Help or Ready actions as noted; players may swap who takes which beat if they plan it before entering the kill zone.

h4. Brassmeade Bridge — seven-beat escalation

# *The club swings* — Lead PC. Passing under the colossus or the first brass footfall shears a display pin; the club sweeps in its posed arc (inertia and gravity, not a living attack). DEX save DC 15, or Acrobatics DC 15 to dive between the legs. Fail: 3d10 bludgeoning, prone; disadvantage on the next beat's first save.
# *The club detaches and falls* — Second PC. The sweep tears the club free; it drops along the bridge approach and blocks or bounds through the lane. DEX DC 15 (Small/Tiny +2 to DC) or STR Athletics DC 14 to shove the haft aside and keep a gap open. Fail: 2d10 bludgeoning, restrained until STR DC 14 escape.
# *Colossus collapse* — Third PC. Pelvis and ribs avalanche along the approach; cart-sized bones roll. Same checks as beat 2. Fail: 2d10 bludgeoning, restrained until STR DC 14 escape.
# *Brassmeade gaps* — Fourth PC. Impacts crack the bridge; only staggered brass plates remain over the chasm. Acrobatics DC 15 (half speed) or STR Athletics DC 16 plate-to-plate while the span tilts. Fail: fall 30–40 ft to diorama floor below (4d6 bludgeoning; stuck among display skeletons until rescued).
# *Counterweight cascade* — Fifth PC. The giant was rigged: brass cables run to hidden counterweights. Cables whine; a pallet of ingots and ballast drops along the chasm wall and **swings** across the bridge mouth (pendulum, two passes, then fouled on broken plate). DEX DC 15 to flatten against the giant's shin or bridge rail, or Acrobatics DC 15 to duck under between swings. Ally may shove prone (advantage) or anchor rope to a fallen rib (cover for one swing). Fail: 3d8 bludgeoning, pushed 10 ft toward the chasm edge.
# *The Great Divide* - Sixth PC. The falling counterweights fully detach the central panels. The PCs have to either leap across the gap or try to balance on the balustrades.
# *Rank collapse* — Seventh PC. Shockwave knocks posed armies into the silver path — skulls, spears, shields sliding. Each PC DEX DC 13 or go prone; this PC also STR Athletics DC 14 to brace a fallen Tellerite pike and keep a lane. Fail: lane blocked; lose a round clearing or repeat saves.
# *Spear-fence slide* — All PCs. Thronged Tellerite skeletons around the exit were pegged in a rearguard wedge; rank collapse shears the pegs and the mass **slides toward the doorway**, spears forward, narrowing the exit to a 2–3 ft gap. Acrobatics DC 16 to thread the gap before it closes, or Athletics DC 15 to vault and dive (Medium or larger: use the higher DC). Gap shrinks one more round — second PC through DC 14 this round, DC 17 if they wait. Fail: 2d8 piercing, restrained; allies STR Athletics DC 14 to pry free before the wedge jams (then group STR DC 16 to shift one bundle).

**Aftermath:** Catastrophic exhibit damage; caretakers prioritise containment and retrieval over a fair fight — good hook into the next deep-archive rooms. Tune DCs for party level (~5–6); plate imposes +1 DC on Stealth-only beats elsewhere, not on these structural saves.

h3. Founding of the Magedom of Houd

**Exhibit type:** Automated historical presentation — not a combat arena. Kingdom panels on the magical equivalent of hydraulics rise and fall to spotlight whichever realm the narration is discussing. A **visitor lane** (inset brass strip with low rails and floor markers: *REMAIN ON GUIDED PATH*) runs from the entrance to the exit under the golden arch. The founding **tableau** on the central dais is static props (mixed-material delegates around a table); the **story is told by the map and the voice**, not by walking on kingdom borders.

**Why it malfunctions:** Brassmeade damage tripped a **stress flag** in this wing; the presentation stack is old; the Anathemists **tampered with the path** on their way to the [[Quarantine Zone | Zone]]. Result: narration **desyncs** from the lifts, centuries of politics **overlap**, and the safe route is **trapped**.

bq). The floor ahead of you is carved into a relief map of northern [[Artresh | Artresh]]. It is a patchwork of various stones and metals, each section trimmed in bronze where it abuts its neighbour. At the centre stands a raised dias with a tableau - a score of statues each made of a different material stand around a table. Ona plinth behind each, facing out into the room, is a skull. To your right, behind a waist-high reproduction of the [[Arkay Mountain Range | Arkay Mountains]] you can see an elaborate golden arch, its decorative ornamentation forming the silhouette of the city of [[Sindrel | Sindrel]]. A pathway winds its way around the room, knee-high railings wending between the borders of inlaid kingdoms as they perform almost a full circuit before terminating at the golden archway.

As they step onto the path:

bq). As your foot touches the surface, you feel a dull rumbling beneath you. Magical illumination stutters to weak and uncertain life behind filigreed grilles in the walls, and the patchwork of stone kingdoms grindingly lowers several feet leaving the path above them.
<br>
At the central dias, the skull facing you is wreathed in sickly necromantic flame. A voice echoes over the chamber - "Welcome to the unification of the northern kingdoms under the Mage Lord Council. Our story begins with the collapse of the maritime trade empire of the Venatici city state..." a section of the map to your left rises jerkily to the height of the path, while a flickering spotlight illuminates an area slighty to its right.


h4. The presentation (erratic)

Run as **six beats**, ~one round each unless the party dawdles. On each beat the **named region lifts, sinks, or tilts** (typically 3–6 ft change at edges; centre dais stays fixed). After Brassmeade + sabotage, roll **1d6** on the beat or pick **wrong kingdom** occasionally so the room feels broken.

# *Origins* - With the Venatici trade empire in ruins, supplies that were once plentiful become scarce. Strife and minor local wars becomes the norm.
# *The Initial Efforts* - The Soulwind Institute, centre of magical learning, maintains a network of magical communication with their  sister institutions throughout the continent. This allows them to coordinate efforts between regions. The success of these efforts provide the good will to build a base of political power that ultimately gives them control of Sindrel itself.
# *The First Alliance* - Sindrel forms an alliance with the neighbouring states of [TODO]. Opposition grows from distant regions.
# *Founding* — The first mage lords (statues at the table) formally begin the parliament of mages - a unified government ruling the founding member states of the Magedom. Narration: arguments, syncretisation [[The Gods | religion]], council charter.
# *Expansion* — Border kingdoms pulse up and down like breathing as the narration details squables, wars, treaties, and marriages. One by one, the satellite kingdoms are absorbed into the expanding Magedom. Standardised coinage seems to play a large role.
# *Peace* - The Magedom hits a steady state. The large Tellerite empire to the south has no interest in joining, so the governance turns inward. The endless litiny of petty disagreements continues, but the Magedom as a whole is able to resolve them peacefully.
# *Looming threat* - On a slightly jingoistic note, the final narrator discusses fears that the expansionist tellerites will attempt to annex the southern states of the Magedom.

**Optional player knowledge:** History DC 12 on any beat predicts **which kingdom the voice meant to highlight** — useful if they cross off-path (see below), not required on the visitor lane.

h4. Anathemist traps (on the guided path only)

The cell booby-trapped **where investigators would walk**, not the whole map — delay pursuers, maim interlopers, avoid wasting charges on the whole hall. Their own route went over the least historically important kingdoms (least likely to move) - discoverable by close inspection if they know what they're looking for.

| Trap | Where | Tell | Disarm / bypass |
|------|--------|------|------------------|
| **Powder seam** | Lane where it crosses a seam (first third of room) | Perception DC 14: grit, smell, fresh scrape | Thieves' Tools DC 15; or **skip** by jumping the seam on a beat when that tile is **down** (timed, DEX DC 13) |
| **Trip wire** | Mid-lane, at a curve. The wire is genuinely designed only to trip, the barrier has been carefully cut and replaced - putting weight on it shears off the top leaving sharpened uprights. | Investigation DC 15finds the wire | |
| **False chalk** | Arrow pointing **off** lane toward “shortcut” | Investigation DC 13 vs fake marker - the anathemists don't use arrows to mark their routes | Ignore; off-lane is shifting map hazard centered on Goatheim.|
| **Last arch charge** (optional, deadly) | Brass threshold before exit arch | Perception DC 15 if searching exit | Tools DC 16; fail = 3d6 fire + noise; **disarm strongly preferred** |

Traps are **mundane** ([[Anathemists | Anathemist]] kit), keyed to **presentation motion** where noted — the powder seam fires when the adjacent kingdom **rises** on beat 2 or 4.

h4. Choice: path or map

**Stay on the visitor lane** — Face traps one by one (or speed-run and eat consequences). Map motion is **minor** under the brass strip (vibration, sudden 1 ft drop at a seam). Suitable for methodical play and **Silrie / Ayr** clearing while armoured PCs advance.

**Leave the lane** — Avoid traps entirely, but **every presentation beat** threatens anyone on moving kingdom tiles:

* When a highlighted kingdom **rises** under a PC: DEX save DC 14 or fall prone; edge of tile = DEX DC 15 or slide 10 ft (into trench between pistons, 2d6 bludgeoning).
* When a kingdom **drops** while occupied: DEX DC 14 or fall **into the bed** (10 ft, 2d6, restrained in machinery until STR DC 14 escape; take 1d6 bludgeoning if the next beat **raises** the same tile).
* **Predicting the beat:** History DC 12 or Perception DC 14 (watch for initial grinding against friction) grants **advantage** on the next save on the map.

**Mixed column:** Allowed — e.g. two disarmers on lane, rest shortcut across **one** rising kingdom during beat 3 with a timed dash. Anathemists used the lane; **Marceline's route** may show **scuff marks off-path** only where tiles were down during their timing (Investigation DC 14 to spot the pattern — intel, not mandatory).

h4. Emergency sink

If the loop **resets** (beat 6) or the party **triggers two traps**, run a **maintenance sink**: all map panels except the dais and visitor lane drop **1 ft** over 6 seconds. Anyone off-path on a sinking tile: DEX DC 15. Fail: trapped in piston well until allies pull (Athletics DC 14) or next beat raises that tile (2d6 crush).

**Aftermath:** Presentation aborts into **static mode** — voice dead, lights low, hydraulics ticking. Caretakers already inbound from Brassmeade; this room adds **sabotage on record** if traps detonate. Exit: through the arch to **Hall of Failed Resurrections**.


h3. The Hall of Failed Resurrections

bq). The corridor ends at a pair of white doors, their surfaces engraved with dozens of names.

bq). Beyond is a long, cold gallery of glass cases. Unlike the other halls of the archive's strange museum, there is more on display here than mere bone - the cold is a preservative measure.
<br>
The bodies within the cases are strange. No two are alike, and none look wholly correct. One corpse casts two shadows in the flickering light of your torch. Another has been reassembled with the bones of three different species. A woman sits upright beneath a veil, her flesh perfectly preserved on one side and reduced to clean ivory on the other.
<br>
Every case bears a narrow brass plate: a name and three dates atop a dense block of text. Three cases stand empty. Winding between the cases you can see the characteristic worn path of an animate skeletal caretaker.

h4. Examining the cases.

The plaque for each case give the name of the individual (if known), the dates of their birth and death (if known) and the date of their attempted resurrection. The text below details the magics used (in a necromantic notation none of the party read) and the result, invariably a failure and often involving a bodycount.

Two of the three empty cases correspond to the two teaching skeletons at Fallowhurst. These are two of the earliest examples, and the least destructive failures. Both bear an additional plaque reading *ON LOAN — FALLOWHURST COLLEGE OF MEDICINAL STUDIES.*

The dragonborn was an attempt at soulbinding - the same technology that would ultimately power the archives (can be peaced together from diagrams of a binding circle - a predecessor of the mark that DW and Wilrin now bear on their forearms).

bq(in-world-document). Specimin A-FR-0881 Personal Name Unkown <br>?–?–3221
<br>
An early experiment in Soulbinding.
<br>
Assessment: Binding persisted for 14.3s before decaying. Note the lack of stabilising reciprocal terms in the containment heuristic.

The elven skeleton was a variant on the lich equation - an attempt to redirect the body's unused heat and magic into the phylactory in place of the souls it would otherwise need to consume. It resulted in a runaway reaction where the spell rapidly consumed _all_ heat and magic from the subject, killing them quickly and horribly and reducing the phylactory to dust.

bq(in-world-document). Andronyx Zrovkas <br>3018–3231–3231
<br>
Attempted lichdom variant using entropic bodily thaumic and thermal byproducts to empower the phylactoric vessil in place of soul consumption.
<br>
Assessment: An unbalanced remainder in the modified Lichdom Equation resulted in endothaumic runaway - unchecked draw from the phylactoric vessel drained the subject's vitality leading to bodily expiration after 3.14s and complete soul dissolution after 6.12s.

The final empty case is the newest. The party recognise the name- Carlos Winterborne. The dates are 3213, 3238, and 3238. The caption (following the necromantic notation) reads

bq(in-world-document). Carlos Winterborne<br>3213-3238-3238
<br>
A novel and extreme variation upon the Lichdom Equation, substituting the mortally wounded subject themselves in place of the external phylacteric vessel. The intended outcome was to soulbind the subject unto their own corpus, whilst rendering the latter inviolable. Additional protective terms were intended to ensnare incidental magics to sustain the binding, affording both protection and freedom from the classical lich's dependence upon the consumption of external vitality.
<br>
Assessment: Failure of the order of operations — the corpus, having been rendered inviolable, rejected the soulbind. The rebound is understood to have consumed the entirety of Fallowhurst College.


In a secondary, temporary plaque:

bq). Procurement pending.

The curators classify Wilrin's skull as an overdue exhibit. If he refuses to surrender it, a caretaker may begin the return procedure; if he presents himself as the skull's current bearer, the archive may instead demand an explanation, a loan extension, or a replacement accession.

h3. The Hall of Plagues

bq). The double doors at the end of the corridor stand ajar. As you approach your light glints on glass in the room beyond. Strangely, the doorframe is rimmed with frost.

The door was sealed when the room detected a breach. The anathemists forced it open.

bq). The air changes before the door is fully open. It smells of vinegar, old linen, and something sweet that has gone wrong. Frost rims the threshold.
<br>
The gallery beyond is flanked by rows of tall containers of greenish glass. Within each floats a corpse, in various stages of decay but uniformly twisted and misshapen by disease. The walls behind them are lined with glass vessels housing smaller specimens, rats and other vermin, body parts, and individual organs.
<br>
At the centre of the room, a black-robed skeleton is slumped behind a lecturn.

Brass plaques identify the outbreaks by name, location, and mortality. Several list no cure. Others record cures that were discovered too late to matter. Along the walls, rows of sealed jars contain blackened lungs, pale nodules, bundles of hair, and things too small to identify. Each jar is marked with a warning in three different scripts.

Disease names:
* *Tellerin Violet* - purple rash along spell-scars; Houd blamed Tellarin “curse” — likely venereal misfiled. Spreads on contact.
* *Ivory Creep* - Flesh to bone from extremities inward; rare negative reaction to bone-mending healing spells.
* *The Last Count / Bleenactun* - patients are compelled to count aloud until they cannot. No known cure. All patients, regardless of language or education, include 'bleen' between 6 and 7.
* *Dimming Pox* - Fever resulting from spellcasting draining the caster's magic. Extreme cases draw on life energy itself, causing atrophy of the organs. Thought to be a plague due to its gradual spread from the south, actually an early sign of the death of magic.
* *Second Breath* - A nasty combination of a healing spell trying to help with breathing with a strain of viral pneumonia. The spell bound to the virus, causing all those infected to increasingly inflate their lungs, ultimately suffocating as the cannot expell dead air. Cure: forced exhilation to clear the corrupted air from the lungs.
* *Venatici Rot* - Venatici naval yards; gangrene of the limb from contaminated ballast water

The anathemists didn't stop here - they passed through as quickly as possible hoping that their armour would shield them from any risk. The party can find places where the reliquaries have been recently moved to clear a path.

The skeleton is a caretaker, but at some stage it fell against the lectern damaging the inscription on its sternum. Restoring it activates the skeleton, who will immediately identify a containment breach and trigger containment.

Opening a reliquary or disturbing a sealed display should trigger the caretakers' contamination protocol.

Possible escalating procedures:
* Frost seals the doors.
* Green glass partitions slide into place, dividing the party.
* Preservative vapour begins filling one section of the room.
* The physician prepares to open a secondary reliquary because it believes the first diagnosis is incomplete.

The party needs to identify the failing exhibit and restore containment. They could:
* Answer the physician’s questions precisely.
* Match the symptoms and exposure details to the correct plague record.
* Find the old containment instructions on the relevant plaque.
* Repair, reinforce, or isolate the damaged seal.
* Convince the system that the party’s magical injuries or necromantic protection are not infectious symptoms.

If they fail, the disease should escape as a developing crisis rather than an immediate combat encounter: perhaps a localised cloud, contaminated clothing, or an infected specimen escaping into the archive. The party then has to decide whether to seal themselves in, risk spreading it, or trigger a wider quarantine.


h3. The Extinct Menagerie

bq). The chamber opens into a space of cathedral scale. Rows of columns march in orderly fashion toward distant walls, above you the ceiling is lost in shadows. Staircases wind around them, granting access to walkways and galleries high above. The base of each column is ringed with display cases. You can just make out the nearest - beneath the glass serrated teeth longer than a man's arm are neatly arrayed on black velvet.
<br>
Between the columns, towering above you are colossal forms - the skeletons of creatures of immense size. To your left a 40-foot long biped has been posed as if rounding the column towards you. The jaw of its massive skull is hinged open, rows of predator teeth on full display as if the beast were an instant away from bringing those dagger-like points down upon you. Opposite it, the body of a quadruped is largely hidden behind a column but you can follow the curve of its spine from the tip of a tail next to the door as it rises in a sinuous curve past the walkways above you. It's surprisingly small skull is almost lost in the darkness easily 80 feet from the floor below. Other forms of similar scale can be seen extending out into the shadows of the room.
<br>
The shifting light as you enter the room almost makes the skeletons look as though they're moving. Is there a shift in the air? The distant sound of bone grinding against stone at the edge of your hearing? Or is it just your imagination.

One of the 'staircases' is actually a titanoboa skeleton resurrected by the necromantic fallout and coiled around the pillar. The anathemists had one near miss, then charted a route that avoided it (chalk marks are findable if the party are looking). It'll mostly just be curious, but will defend itself if they attack.

The party can navigate the walkways, restricting the snake's movement but also their own.



h3. The Quarantine Zone and the Sacrum

bq). The route makes its way up a grand and winding staircase. As you climb, you pass alcoves containing Samhain-era props (evacuation bell, empty Red Mourning reliquary, processional skull-mask — see *Grand stair alcoves* above). Finally you find yourselves once again in the familiar surroundings of the public hall of an osteomantic archive — austere rows of pews around a central dias inlaid with what you now know to be an excarnation spell. The room is deserted and eerily silent; the gentle creak as the open doors shift in the breeze is the only sound.

The party emerges from the deep archives into the Quarantine Zone. The Zone should feel like a dead city preserved at the instant of catastrophe, with no bodies despite every other detail remaining in place.

bq). You emerge into a silent street, the sun obscured by a wall of low, dense cloud that makes the afternoon feel like the depths of evening. The street looks like any other in sindrel, but completely deserted. The silence hangs oppressively over everything.

Any houses they search are untouched, and deserted. Food remains on plates where it left - desiccated rather than rotted, not even bacteria live in this dead place. There are no bodies.

bq). The further you go, the more the schizophrenic architecture of Sindrel gives way to an unfamiliar pattern. Dark stone and pointed arches, gargoyles worn smooth by centuries of rain, skull motifs appearing with greater and greater regularity. Residential streets give way to more formal ceremonial patterns - plazas, audatoria, and mausoleums dominate.

At the Sacrum:

* The party finds the Anathemists' former staging ground.
* Evidence includes chemical traces, charcoal residue, armour-preparation marks, copied-route notes, and signs of repeated disguised movement.
* The stockpile contains the remaining explosives and materials for the Dockward operation.
* Nielsenn's corpse lies abandoned in the building. His spirit remains nearby, trapped by the ambient necromancy, and can tell the party what happened during the Ditchquer heist.
* Reinforcements or surviving merchants may still be present and prepared to fight.
* The Anathemists can deliver their speech about Sindrel's rot and House Oestra's machinations before attempting to escape, bargain, or force a confrontation.

bq). You know the building before anyone names it. It sits at the centre of the Quarantine Zone like a verdict - flying buttresses of black stone, pointed arches stacked upon pointed arches, gargoyles festoon the lintels.
<br>
The doors are grand. The windows are narrow.. No lamp burns at the entrance. The heart of the eighth house is dark, and cold, and hungry.

Interior:

bq). The air is dry and colder than the Zone outside — preserved, not fresh. Corridors branch into memorial galleries where niches wait in ranks, not the compact alcoves of the catacombs but spaces in which a full skeleton could stand. Floors are black polished stone. Vaulting draws the eye upward into shadow, then denies it a resting place. Every room feels like the antechamber to a larger grief.
<br>
There is no bustle of a living headquarters — no clerks, no petitioners, no polite necromantic commerce. What remains is worse than emptiness: the sense of duties half-finished. The necromantic pressure is strongest here — not the diffuse wrongness of the streets, but a focused weight, as though the building itself were still trying to complete a ritual interrupted a thousand years ago.
<br>
For the first time in this place of death you find actual evidence of the dead - a dessicated corpse sits slumped against one wall.

The corpse is [[:nielsenn | Nielsenn]], [[House Mabon | House Mabon]] adjutant locksmith whom the Anathemists kidnapped, used, and murdered. Approaching him causes his ghost to manifest — see *The Sacrum Entrance* (Session 19 plan) for dated narration and asks.
