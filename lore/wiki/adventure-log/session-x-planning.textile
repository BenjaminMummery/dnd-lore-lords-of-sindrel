h2. The Quarantine Zone

h3. Entrance from the Wallarch Breach

bq). The iron boundary rises in a line too straight to be natural: black metal banded with bronze, ward-glyphs repeating at intervals no craftsman would choose without instruction, maintenance scaffolds clinging to the face like lichen on a wound that refuses to close.
<br>
Down a side street, around the back of a warehouse, through a gap between buildings that was never meant to be a path, gradually the crowded bustle of Wallarch fades into the background and you find your way to the  breach. A break in the wall, a narrow crack that runs vertically, a hair's breadth at the top, barely wider enough for a person at the base. It's a squeeze. Deathwalker needs to turn sideways.
<br>
On the other side, the Quarantine Zone stretches away. Deserted streets. Houses whose blank windows regard you like empty eye sockets. All shrouded in deathly silence. Nothing moves, not a bird, not a rat, not a single sign of life can be seen.
<br>
Standing within it, you understand why the Quarantine Zone is the stuff of horror stories and childish nightmares. The air has a weight to it - not heat, not cold, but pressure at the temples, as though the district ahead were watching you from directions geometry does not admit. Your skin registers it before your mind names it: a wrong place. A dead place.

h3. Within

bq). The further you go, the more the schizophrenic architecture of Sindrel gives way to an unfamiliar pattern. Dark stone and pointed arches, gargoyles worn smooth by centuries of bad weather and worse magic, skull motifs chosen with the restraint of people who thought they were being tasteful. Streets lie where they fell: courts, ossuary gates, memory-archives with their doors hanging open, hospitals and academies and civic halls all arranged with the terrible patience of a civilisation that believed death was a transition, not an ending.
<br>
That belief lingers in the stones. The Zone feels less abandoned than unresolved — as though someone left in the middle of a sentence and the punctuation never arrived. In places the necromantic residue is visible: a faint glow on lintels, frost that is not cold, dust that arranges itself into ridges no wind could have made.
<br>
Somewhere ahead — at the heart of the wound, where the public story says the [[The Eighth House | Eighth House]] died — the architecture grows denser, more deliberate, more official. If the Zone is a scar, that is the place the blade went deepest.

Wisdom check - the thing you _should_ be seeing but aren't. Every detail of the quarantine zone remains as it was left in a single instant (the food has desiccated rather than rotting - not even bacteria lives here). But there are no bodies.

h2. The Sacrum


bq). You know the building before anyone names it. The Sacrum.
<br>
It sits at the centre of the Quarantine Zone like a verdict - flying buttresses of black stone, pointed arches stacked upon pointed arches, gargoyles festoon the lintels. Skull motifs here are not decoration; they are punctuation. A grand sprawling mass of halls, archives, and spires.
<br>
The doors are grand. The windows are narrow.. No lamp burns at the entrance. The Sacrum is dark, and cold, and hungry.


h3. Interior

bq). The air is dry and colder than the Zone outside — preserved, not fresh. Corridors branch into memorial galleries where niches wait in ranks, not the compact alcoves of the catacombs but spaces in which a full skeleton could stand. Floors are black polished stone. Vaulting draws the eye upward into shadow, then denies it a resting place. Every room feels like the antechamber to a larger grief.
<br>
There is no bustle of a living headquarters — no clerks, no petitioners, no polite necromantic commerce. What remains is worse than emptiness: the sense of duties half-finished. The necromantic pressure is strongest here — not the diffuse wrongness of the streets, but a focused weight, as though the building itself were still trying to complete a ritual interrupted a thousand years ago.

The party will need magical protection to get here. Luckily, Kelferria has plenty to hand.

h2. The Bonded Garden

bq). The Canted Terrace does not announce itself. It sidesteps.
<br>
The walk up is all polished steps and iron lamps, ceremonial gutters, and carved stonework. The district sits high enough to be respectable and at an angle to everything fashionable - walled, screened, and spoken of in the careful tones people use for places they are not supposed to enjoy looking at. Coachmen know the routes. Factors mention addresses without blushing. Merrylane, somewhere beyond the sightline tricks, pretends not to know which way you turned.
<br>
Incense reaches you before the name does - amber, musk, something sweet enough to make you forget what quarter you are in. Then the sign: modest on purpose, gilt on black lacquer. The Bound Garden. Beneath it, in smaller script: Private Repose. By Introduction.
<br>
No painted nymphs. No winking cherubs. Only a pierced bronze screen set into the facade, and lamplight falling through arabesque cutouts to spill across the Terrace walk in patterns too pretty to be innocent. Black marble steps. Gold-threaded tiles glimpsed through smoke-coloured veils. 

At the door: if they have no introduction, an attendant may appear behind the screen — flawless, unhurried, attentive in a way that feels less like service and more like being seen through. Discreet admission is introduction, House credit, or a vouched name.

Optional one-liner if someone asks what kind of place it is: "The sort of club respectable people call a club, and everyone else calls none of their business."

bq). The door admits you into warmth that has been arranged, not accumulated.
<br>
Incense first - amber, musk, sweetness. Pierced screens throw latticed shadow across black polished floors. Through them you can see a suggestion of bodies moving in a smoky haze - never glimpsed clearly, always tantalisingly vague. Lamplight is low and deliberate. Gold filigree catches on every latch, every hinge, every frame — nothing shabby, nothing hurried, nothing that looks as though it has ever been surprised by a guest.
<br>
Attendants appear when needed and never when they are not: flawless, unhurried, attentive in a way that feels less like service and more like being seen through. Their smiles are perfect. Their silence is professional.
<br>
The rooms open off the corridor like small palaces - cushions piled in calculated disorder, low lamps, reflecting pools no larger than a washbasin, veils of smoke-coloured silk drawn against walls. Somewhere deeper in the building, very faintly, you catch the suggestion of music, as if someone had remembered that pleasure ought to have a soundtrack.
<br>
If you ever saw a price list, it would be written in the same calm hand as a summoning contract. The Bound Garden sells discretion the way other houses sell wine: expensively, and with the unspoken understanding that you are not to ask what happens in the cellar when the last lamp goes out.

Potential lead the party might follow before talking to Silas. The Bonded Garden summons Succubi to fulfil its clients' desires.

h2. The Summons

When the party finally answer the summons, they get to approach the Leageur from without rather than within.

bq). The Leageur occupies an entire walled district of Sindrel’s ground level like a declaration of intent. While the headquarters of other Great Houses might resemble temples, libraries, palaces or occasionally architectural nervous breakdowns, the Leageur looks unmistakably like a fortress.
<br>
At least, it does after a second glance.
<br>
The first glance tends to be distracted by the marble.
<br>
House Oestra have covered the exterior of the complex in fluted columns, soaring domes and enough crimson pennants to suggest that a very patriotic hurricane has recently passed through. White marble gleams in the sunlight. Bronze doors tower overhead. Heroic statuary adorns every approach. The entire ensemble projects the reassuring message that civilisation was grand, noble and entirely in control of events.
<br>
The second glance noticed the murder holes.
<br>
The third noticed the iron gates.
<br>
By the fourth, you realise that the marble is largely decorative and that nearly every attractive feature concels something designed to repel a siege.
<br>
The broad ceremonial avenues narrow abruptly at strategic points. Grand staircases can be sealed by portcullises hidden within ornamental arches. The elegant colonnades stand before walls thick enough to stop artillery, constructed from magically reinforced volcanic concrete and merely pretending to be marble for social reasons.
<br>
Beyond the walls lie a series of vast open spaces whose scale seems almost absurd. Parade grounds stretch across acres of stone. Assembly halls large enough to accommodate entire armies stand empty beneath colossal domes. Armouries occupy wings grander than most noble palaces.
<br>
And yet there are remarkably few people.
<br>
The result is a headquarters that feels less inhabited than prepared. Every courtyard appears ready for a parade. Every hall seems moments away from hosting a senate session. Every wall gives the impression that a war could begin at any moment.
<br>
At the very centre of the Leageur stands the Senate Hall of Oestra, crowned by its vast dome and surrounded by enough fortifications to survive the collapse of civilisation several times over.

h3. The Senate Chamber

bq). The Senate chamber of the Leageur is built to make visitors feel small on purpose: White stone, bronze Oestran sunburst, ranks of empty benches climbing into shadow. House guards stand at the walls with the bored competence of men who have never needed to be imaginative. At the far end of an expanse of polished marble floor stands a grand throne of dark iron atop a tiered dais.
<br>
[[:commander-locke | Prefect Locke]] waits by the Triumphal door — half-plate, eyepatch, arms folded, visibly wishing to be anywhere else.
<br>
[[:silas-oestra | Silas Oestra]] does not wait. He paces the floor before the dais, hand clenched on the hilt of his sword, knuckles white as bone. For all his rank, the heir of House Oestra wears his authority with palpable discomfort. A human who cannot quite have reached thirty: fair-skinned, lean, restless, with dark hair worn longer than strict [[House Oestra | Oestra]] fashion permits and a clean-shaven jaw that still looks a little unfinished. He wears Oestra finery as though armour could supply what birth has not yet granted — blackened breastplate over crimson cloth, bronze at the shoulders, long sword worn openly — every piece correct, nothing quite settled. Chest out, chin high, voice slightly nasal and much too fast, as if volume and procedure might stand in for respect. He speaks before you have fully stopped moving.

h3. Silas

bq). "There you are. At last. Why am I not surprised that you exhibit such reluctance to come when invited after seeming so eager to go poking around when unwelcome? It is customary to kneel, incidentally."


bq). "Very well. I am given to understand that you were commissioned by a Hexine Circle to investigate the leak of privileged information, and by extension the series of so called sabotages that have plagued the Great HOuses. Instead you have abused that authority to conduct a campaign of trespass and intimidation against **mine**.
<br>
You were caught emerging from regions of this House you had no authority nor permission to enter, and in which your very presence was an act of desecration against the lineage of this house, my lineage. Were that not enough, you proceeded to exploit the poor judgement of one of my prefects in order to muscle your way into essential and secret military infrastructure. And in pursuit of what, precisely? [[House Grimbolg | Grimbolg]] lost a silo. [[House Beltus | Beltus]] lost a caller to his own demon. [[House Lithra | Lithra]] caused an international embarrassment. Nothing that cannot be explained by lax discipline and carelessness. Meanwhile [[House Meness | Meness]] cannot keep our core infrastructure secure. And yet for some reason you appear to be focussed on _us_.
<br>
We suffered **no such incidents**. None. Our security held. Our people held. That is not a mystery. That is proof that whatever you are hunting, it is **not here**.
<br>
So explain to me — politely, if you can manage it — why my House keeps paying for your indiscretions.

h4. Culmination

bq). "Silence! I will not be lied to in my own hall. Were you members of my house I would have you executed for such insolence. Locke may have bought into your ridiculous tale, and believe me he will be suitably disciplined when may father returns, but I am not so easily misled. Were it up to me you would have no investigatorial mandate whatsoever - Founder's teeth all of this on the back of a few accidents, a failed bombing, and one anathemist saying 'Kosmios', my father would _weep_ to see how far we have fallen."
<br>
You see Locke stiffen. An odd expression passes across Silas's face for a second before he continues.
<br>
"The [[Hexine Circle | Hexine Circle]] commissioned you to find **spies**, not to invade my House. There is a difference, and I suggest you learn it before my father's patience - and mine - is exhausted.
<br>
You will investigate criminals, smugglers, the sort of vermin who nest in  [[The Wallarch District | Wallarch]] dives. That is useful work. Breaking archive seals and bullying armourers is not investigation. It is **harassment**, and I will not have it recorded that House Oestra tolerated it.
<br>
Any further transgression of your bounds will be treated as an attack against the city, is that understood?"
