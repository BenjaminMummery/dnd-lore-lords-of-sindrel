An upper-tier house of negotiable affection on [[The Canted Terrace | the Canted Terrace]] — illicit in letter, tolerated in practice, and spoken of by anyone who matters as though it were a respectable private club that happens to require an introduction. Foreign factors, visiting heirs, and Sindrellians with expense accounts treat it as part of the city's unwritten hospitality. The [[Hexine Circle | Hexine Circle]] does not advertise it; neither does it shut it down.

[[:silas-oestra | Silas Oestra]] was banned from the premises in early 4218 SC after an incident the proprietors describe, with admirable vagueness, as _a failure of manners toward the staff_.[1]

h3. Exterior

bq). The sign is modest on purpose: gilt letters on black lacquer, _The Bound Garden_, and beneath them, in smaller script, _Private Repose. By Introduction._ No painted nymphs, no winking cherubs — only a pierced bronze screen set into the facade so that lamplight falls through arabesque cutouts and spills onto the Terrace walk in patterns too pretty to be innocent.
<br>
The building is [[House Beltus | Beltus]]-leaning in its bones whether or not anyone admits the connection: black marble steps, gold-threaded tiles, smoke-coloured veils behind the screen, and a door that opens before your hand quite reaches it. Coachmen know the address. Factors mention it without blushing. Merrylane residents pretend not to know which direction it lies.

h3. Interior

bq). Incense first — amber, musk, something sweet enough to make you forget what district you are in. Corridors turn where straight lines would be easier; pierced screens throw latticed shadow across black polished floors. Attendants appear when needed and never when they are not: flawless, unhurried, attentive in a way that feels less like service and more like being _seen through_. The rooms are small palaces — cushions, low lamps, reflecting pools no larger than a washbasin, gold filigree on every latch. Nothing is shabby. Nothing is hurried. The price list, if you ever see one, is written in the same calm hand as a summoning contract.

h2. Reputation

* **Open secret** — not on civic maps; universally known among the upper tiers.
* **Discreet admission** — introduction, House credit, or a name vouched for by an existing client.
* **Upper-tier clientele** — diplomats, merchant princes, bored heirs, and Factors who prefer their indulgences screened from [[Merrylane | Merrylane]] windows.
* **Staff** — the house maintains that its attendants are _bound for the evening_ and _released at dawn_. No employment contracts are filed with the city. No one asks follow-up questions.

h2. Overview

Covert [[House Beltus | House Beltus]] revenue and influence operation. Public proprietor is a shell Caller; senior Beltus Factors hold the real keys. No human sex workers on the roster — clients are attended by **summoned succubi** (or incubi on request), bound for the appointment and dismissed afterward. Beltus treats this as disciplined conjuration: circles, witnesses among senior staff, banishment on the hour. The house is illegal; the House considers it **regulated**.

**Why here:** [[The Canted Terrace | The Canted Terrace]] is already screened from [[Merrylane | Merrylane]], respectable enough for foreign clients, close to [[Beltan Institute | Beltan Institute]] expertise if a binding slips. An open secret suits Beltus — visible enough to collect kompromat, deniable enough to disavow.

**Silas:** banned after he attempted to **command** an attendant as though she were Oestra levy infantry. The binding held; the proprietors did not forgive the insult to their craft.

**Investigation hooks:** succubus circles resemble the vandalised page in [[:caller-georg | Caller Georg]]'s grimoire; sulphur receipts; a Factor's ledger listing _garden maintenance_; clients who remember more than they should after dismissal ( botched banishment ).
